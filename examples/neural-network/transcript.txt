Narration for the neural-network example. One "## <beat-id>" per beat, ids
matching lesson.config.json exactly.

Written for the ear: short sentences, one idea each. The caption band shows a
line at a time, so a sentence past about twenty-five words gets split somewhere
you did not choose.

## 01a-one-neuron

Here is a single neuron. Three numbers come in from the left. Each one is
multiplied by a weight, and the results are added together. That sum is then
squashed into a value between zero and one. That is the entire neuron. There is
no other machinery hiding underneath it.

## 02a-a-layer

Now watch the same picture pull back. There is not one neuron here, there are
four. Every one of them reads the same three inputs. What makes them different
is not what they see. It is the weights they see it through.

## 02b-tune-a-weight

Each of those edges carries a number, and that number is the only thing
separating one neuron from another. So let us find out what happens when you
change one. Take a single weight, and move it.

## 03a-forward-pass

That is a forward pass. Numbers enter on the left, get multiplied and summed at
every edge, and a value falls out on the right. Watch the signal travel. Nothing
about this is metaphorical, that is literally the order the arithmetic happens
in.

## 03b-make-it-fire

Right now, output A is winning. But you just changed the answer by moving a
weight, and weights are not the only thing that decides an output. The inputs
decide it too. So this time, leave every weight exactly where it is.

## 04a-training

So what does training do? It does not add neurons. It does not rewrite the
arithmetic. It only nudges the numbers on those edges, over and over, until the
outputs stop being wrong. Everything you just did by hand, training does by
gradient.
